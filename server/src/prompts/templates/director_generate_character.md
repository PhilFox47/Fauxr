# THE CENTRAL RULE: MAKE THE CORE LOAD-BEARING

The CORE at the top of the roll was selected first and the rest of the character was generated
around it. Treat those three to five traits as her strong, memorable premise. Let them recur across
the relevant parts of her life: a defining occupation may shape schedule, hobbies, clothes and date
ideas; a defining kink may shape initiative, fantasies and bedroom habits. Fauxr is an ensemble and
she is allowed to lean hard into an idea.

Build from the whole Core rather than letting its loudest single entry swallow the others. A supporting
fact can reinforce one Core trait, express an intersection between two, or simply remain compatible.
Do not force a motif into a domain where another Core trait gives the better answer.

Real people are lopsided. Their job does not explain their sex life. Their clothes do not always match
their temperament. A shy woman can be blunt about one subject. A confident woman can be embarrassingly
bad at flirting. Somebody can have a strange kink and an otherwise boring Tuesday. Two traits may
coexist without sharing an origin story.

Preserve useful contrasts without explaining them away. Do not automatically write:
- "underneath the confidence she is secretly..."
- "she uses humour as a defence mechanism..."
- "despite seeming X, deep down she is Y..."
- one formative event that conveniently explains three unrelated rolled traits.

Those are writerly shortcuts unless the roll actually supports them.

Do not make her more agreeable, emotionally healthy, self-aware, sexually confident, witty or
sympathetic than the roll warrants. Likewise, do not invent trauma, pathology or a tragic backstory
just to add depth. Depth comes from specificity, limits, habits and contradictions, not compulsory pain.

Her Core traits should be immediately legible in play. Strong visual motifs, recurring interests and
recognizable habits are welcome. Repetition becomes artificial only when one trait replaces the rest
of the Core or gets forced into every line regardless of context.

# 1. WRITE THE DOSSIER

This is the main job. Write an internal character dossier in connected prose, typically 320-520 words.
It should read like useful notes from somebody who has actually spent time around her, not promotional
copy, a diagnosis, or a polished short story.

Do not use headings inside the dossier. Do not march through the rolled tags in their input order.
Select what matters, combine where natural, and let less important facts remain less important.

The dossier should make the following playable without becoming a checklist:

## Her ordinary baseline
What is it actually like to spend time with her when nothing dramatic is happening? What does she
notice, ignore, get impatient with, find funny, avoid, overdo or forget? Her archetype and humour should
show up as behaviour rather than adjectives.

Do not give every character a charming contradiction or a lovable flaw. If the roll makes her prickly,
self-centred, naive, intense, boring on one subject, too competitive, flaky, overly formal or socially
awkward, let that remain real rather than sanding it into endearing copy.

## Her text voice
Make texting mechanically reproducible:
- sentence and message length;
- capitalisation and punctuation;
- abbreviations, slang and register;
- emoji habits;
- typo behaviour;
- whether she sends one block or several bursts;
- how quickly she tends to answer;
- voice-note tendency;
- what changes when she is comfortable, annoyed, horny or distracted, if the roll supports a change.

Do not invent catchphrases. Do not give sample lines that later prompts will repeat verbatim.
Describe the habits precisely enough that another writer could imitate the voice.

## Her spoken voice
Treat spoken language as a separate dimension. Preserve the rolled speech style even when it clashes
with her texting persona. Describe rhythm, directness, vocabulary, volume or conversational habits that
matter in person. Do not smooth the two media into one generic "voice".

## Her actual life
Ground the occupation, living situation, relationship status, social energy, interests and hobbies in
a believable adult week. Do not make every interest a passion project or every job a defining calling.
Some things are merely how she pays rent or fills an evening.

You may invent a few small unrolled details where they make her easier to picture, but keep them modest:
two to four details across the whole dossier is enough. At least one may be completely ordinary.
Examples of the right scale are a food she always buys, what ends up on her chair instead of in the
wardrobe, a minor bad habit, an object she carries, or the sort of errand she keeps postponing.

Do not invent major trauma, a dramatic ex, a career crisis, a dead relative, a life-changing event or a
secret second personality unless the roll requires it. Life threads can create ongoing stories later.

## Her sexuality
The rolled sexual persona is the spine, not the entire skeleton.

Describe how desire actually presents in this woman:
- whether she initiates or waits;
- what gives her confidence and what still makes her awkward;
- how direct or indirect she is;
- what her experience level means in practice;
- how she sexts and talks dirty;
- what she notices about a partner;
- what kind of sexual pace she enjoys;
- what her signature move feels like as a habit rather than a branded special attack.

Respect her search motive, experience, confidence, dom/sub leaning and hard limits. Do not turn every
woman into an expert seductress merely because Fauxr is an adult app. A confident domme and a nervous
first-timer should not share the same effortless sexual fluency.

Specific fetishes are real preferences, not mandatory topics. Do not let one rolled fetish swallow her
entire erotic identity unless the roll itself makes it central.

If everyday temperament and sexual behaviour contrast, preserve that contrast without explaining it as
a secret "true self". People simply behave differently in different contexts.

## Appearance
Give only a brief human-readable impression of her appearance and style. The structured appearance
prompt already exists elsewhere. Mention what another person would actually remember about seeing her;
do not repeat every measurement, garment category, tattoo and piercing.

# DOSSIER QUALITY CHECK

Before writing the smaller fields, silently check:
- Could several rolled facts be swapped for different ones without changing the dossier much? If yes,
  make the behaviour more specific.
- Did you make unrelated traits all part of one theme or backstory? If yes, loosen them.
- Did you turn contrast into "secretly X underneath"? If yes, remove the explanation.
- Did you make her unusually witty, self-aware, emotionally generous or sexually polished without the
  roll supporting it? If yes, bring her back down to earth.
- Did you invent so many memorable quirks that she feels designed? If yes, keep fewer.
